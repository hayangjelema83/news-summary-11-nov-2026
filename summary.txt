Russia and Ukraine ended US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to a prisoner exchange, but no ceasefire or territorial deal, Reuters reported. At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the climate crisis is 'outrunning us', with a 1.5C overshoot now inevitable, proposing a climate justice compact, UN News reported. The US charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots, including one at RAF Fairford, the AP reported; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after threats. Democrat Scott Colom beat Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, the AP projected, becoming the state's first Black senator since 1874; Democrats will hold 55 seats, Alaska undecided. Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, with 11,042 infections and 5,387 deaths, the AP reported, the country's deadliest outbreak and the second-largest on record. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, the Labor Department reported; Asian shares rallied, Taiwan up 3.6%, as CoreWeave's results eased AI-bubble fears, Reuters reported. A Florida judge let Donald Trump's $10bn defamation case against the BBC proceed, Reuters reported on 11 November; the BBC admits the edit was misleading but denies defamation. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia, the AP reported; he served with NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, and the Kremlin denied any involvement. Israeli airstrikes killed three Palestinians in Gaza, including two brothers sheltering in a tent, medics told Reuters. El Salvador's president said Salvadoran and Haitian forces destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters, but the BBC could not verify the claim or reports of about 20 deaths. A city bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people, nine seriously, Reuters reported; the driver said the brakes failed. In the NBA, San Antonio and Boston both rallied from 22 down to win.
